Science Term Guide — open when you need help
Nucleus
The small, dense central region of an atom that contains protons and neutrons.
Electron cloud
The region around the nucleus where electrons are likely to be found.
Relative charge
A comparison of electric charge: proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1.
u / amu
A unit used to describe very small atomic masses. “u” means unified atomic mass unit.
Neutral atom
An atom with equal numbers of protons and electrons, so its overall charge is zero.
Isotope
Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

Final Clue

13 + 14 = 27

What might 27 represent?

27 is the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus. Protons and neutrons together are called nucleons. This relationship becomes important when we define mass number.

Science Term Guide — Day 3
Atomic number
The number of protons in an atom. It identifies the element.
Mass number
The total number of protons and neutrons in one specific isotope.
Nucleons
A collective name for protons and neutrons in the nucleus.
Isotope
A version of an element that has a particular number of neutrons.
Neutral
Having no overall electric charge because proton and electron counts are equal.
Particle count
The number of each type of subatomic particle present in the model.

Science Term Guide — Day 4
Atomic number (Z)
The proton count. Two atoms with different proton counts are different elements.
Mass number (A)
Protons + neutrons in one isotope. It is a whole number.
Average atomic mass
The weighted average of naturally occurring isotopes; it is different from mass number.
Ion
An atom or group with a net charge because electrons have been gained or lost.
Orbital
A region of probability used by the modern model to describe where an electron may be found.

Location: electron cloud surrounding nucleus

An electron does have mass. Its mass is so small compared with proton and neutron masses that it is neglected in basic mass-number calculations. The symbol u means unified atomic mass unit; introductory materials may also use “amu.”
How to use this: Compare charge, approximate mass, and location. Then move to the Atomic Code and Understanding Check. Protons and neutrons account for almost all of an atom’s mass; electrons have mass but contribute very little to the atom’s total mass.

Worked Example · Neutral Aluminum-27

Atomic number: 13

Mass number: 27

Protons = 13

Electrons = 13 because the atom is neutral.

Neutrons = 27 − 13 = 14

Final composition

13 protons

14 neutrons

13 electrons

The 27 is the mass number of this isotope, not aluminum’s decimal average atomic mass.
